Tokyo Travel Guide

Tokyo is the capital city of Japan.

Shibuya Crossing is one of Tokyo's most famous landmarks.
It is known for its extremely busy pedestrian crossing and
large shopping and entertainment areas.

Senso-ji is a historic Buddhist temple located in Asakusa.
It is one of Tokyo's most popular cultural attractions.

Tokyo Skytree is a broadcasting and observation tower.
Visitors can see a large portion of Tokyo from its observation decks.

Meiji Shrine is located near Harajuku.
It is surrounded by a large forest and is dedicated to Emperor Meiji
and Empress Shoken.

Tokyo is well connected by trains and subway systems.
The JR Yamanote Line connects several important areas of the city.

The Toei Subway covers many districts with lines that reach Shinjuku,
Asakusa, Ueno and other major stops.

Tsukiji Outer Market is famous for fresh seafood and street food.
Visitors can eat sushi, tamagoyaki and fruit parfaits at the market stalls.

Omoide Yokocho, also called Memory Lane, is a small izakaya alley near Shinjuku.
It is popular for grilled yakitori, ramen and local craft beer at night.

Ramen culture is strong in Tokyo.
Popular styles include shoyu, miso, tonkotsu and tsukemen.
Otsukaichan Tsukemen in Iidabashi is a famous tonkotsu tsukemen shop.

The Ghibli Museum is located near Mitaka, a short train ride from Tokyo.
It is dedicated to the animations of Studio Ghibli and Hayao Miyazaki.
Tickets must be bought in advance and sell out quickly.

TeamLab Planets is a digital art museum near Tokyo Waterfront.
Visitors walk through water and immersive light installations.

Sumida Park is a long riverside park along the Sumida River.
In April, thousands of cherry blossom trees line the park,
making it one of the best places for hanami, the flower-viewing tradition.

The Chidori Yotei cherry trees bloom near the National Diet building.
They are famous for the pink contrast between the blossoms and the river.

Tokyo Tower is a red-and-white observation tower in Minato.
It is older than Tokyo Skytree and has a classic retro design.

Golden Gai is a tiny bar district near Shinjuku.
More than two hundred small bars share a narrow alley,
each with its own theme and atmosphere.

Tokyo National Museum, located in Ueno, is one of Japan's oldest museums.
It houses thousands of works of classical Japanese and Asian art.

Ueno Park hosts one of Tokyo's largest cherry blossom festivals in spring.
The park also has several museums and the Tokyo Metropolitan Gymnasium.

The annual Tokyo Marathon is one of the world's biggest running events.
The course takes runners through landmarks like Tokyo Tower
and the Imperial Palace Outer Garden.

Tokyo Disneyland and Tokyo DisneySea are located in Disney Resort in Tokyo Bay.
Tokyo DisneySea was the first Disney water-themed park in the world.

The Imperial Palace Outer Garden is a public park in central Tokyo.
It offers lakes, seasonal flowers and the remains of the old Edo Castle moats.

Harajuku is a trendy district known for fashion, street food and Takeshita Street.
People visit for colorful crepes, snacks and unique clothing stores.

Odaiba is a large artificial island in Tokyo Bay.
It has theme parks, museums, beaches and views of Rainbow Bridge.

Shinjuku Gyoen is a large formal garden in central Tokyo.
It combines Japanese, English and French garden styles
and is a popular quiet retreat from the city.

Akihabara is an electric town famous for electronics, anime,
manga and arcade games. It is a major hub for otaku culture.

Ueno is known for its museums, parks and temples.
Banten Doji is one of the largest Buddhist temples in Tokyo,
with a huge bronze Kannon statue.
